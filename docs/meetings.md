# Meetings

You plan meetings in the calendar. In the tool a meeting begins when
somebody starts it; the tool asks those who aren't the circle's
Secretary (or standing in for them) to confirm. Everybody follows on a
shared screen or their own and adds agenda items from their own device;
the page updates by itself. Whoever adds an item owns it; to add one for
somebody else, end it with their name like the agenda shows it, e.g.
"New knives – Ada" (full, first or short name, if only one person in the
meeting has it).

- **Who leads**: the meeting's secretary has the buttons: steps,
  processing items, adopting, elections, attendees, ending
  the meeting; they also change proposals. It's whoever starts the
  meeting: usually the circle's Secretary, if missing a stand-in (the
  Facilitator, then the Circle Leads, constitution 5.3.6); the tool asks
  others to confirm starting it. "Take over" next to the secretary makes
  somebody else the secretary, e.g. when the Secretary arrives; the tool
  asks those who aren't acting as the circle's Secretary to confirm.
- **Following**: the others see the current step and agenda item
  highlighted, a summary of what the proposal changes (updated while the
  secretary amends it), and open it with "Open the proposal".
  In tactical meetings they look at another step on their own screen by
  clicking it (the checklists, metrics and updates show their reviews, the
  other steps nothing of their own), "Back to the meeting" returns.
  While you type or have something open, the page doesn't update; a
  notice at the top says so (not while the secretary amends the proposal).
- **Notes** at the bottom: what doesn't belong to an agenda item, e.g. a
  special meeting's intent. The secretary writes them, they save
  themselves; the outputs mail includes them.
- **Attendees** ("Attendees" in the agenda's heading) are the circle's
  members at the start; the secretary invites others or removes them
  there. "End the meeting" (■ beside it, large in the closing round)
  asks first while items are unprocessed.
- **One meeting per circle**: starting a second one leads to the running
  one.
- **End**: "End the meeting", or by itself three hours after the last
  change. Then the members and attendees get the outputs by mail.

## Tension inbox

Each circle's page has a "Tension inbox" for its next meeting, tactical or
governance: which one it needs often shows only in the meeting. The
circle's members see it and add to it with "Add a tension" (a few words
like on the agenda, a description if needed), also on their own page
with "Tension for the circle" next to each of their circles. The newest
come first with the day they were added, those older than three months
are collapsed ("Show … more"). A **private** one only you see, also in
the meeting: you put it on the agenda yourself. Only you change or delete yours, while
they're in the inbox; once you're no longer a member of the circle, its
Secretary may delete them too (not private ones). Then either:

- **Wait for the meeting**: "From the inbox" below the agenda's field
  puts tensions on the agenda, your own, the secretary's those of the
  attendees, by person ("Select all" checks all of a person's): the
  agenda is built in the meeting by those there (constitution 5.4.4),
  the inbox only notes tensions. The secretary adds somebody's when
  they name them, those of people who aren't there stay in the inbox. The item belongs to whoever wrote
  the tension, its description shows while it's processed; in a
  governance meeting the secretary captures the proposal from it like
  from any tension.
- **Draft a proposal**: "Draft a proposal" next to your tension opens
  "New proposal" with its text. The draft is private until you publish
  it; the tension leaves the inbox (deleting the draft puts it back).

Removing the item from the agenda puts the tension back. When the
meeting ends, the items left unprocessed go into the inbox, also those
added in the meeting; not proposals (they wait for the next meeting) or
elections.

## Governance meeting

"Start a governance meeting" on the circle's page (in its Meetings and in the menu "…", with "All meetings"), every member may do that. Steps: check-in, agenda, closing. The Role Leads of a role start one
on the role's page (in the menu "…") to break it down into roles: adopting the first role
makes it a circle, with them as its Circle Leads. The tool asks first,
cancelling is the default: changing the role itself is proposed in its
circle.

- **Agenda**: a tension in one or two words, or one of your proposals
  (waiting, published async, drafts); the secretary also sees
  "Others' proposals", listed apart (others ask you to bring yours with
  "discuss in a meeting"). "Remove" puts a proposal back: a draft
  stays yours, an async proposal keeps its deadline; once processed in the
  meeting, it's your draft again, to publish for a new round.
- **Process**: the facilitator chooses an agenda item, the secretary
  clicks it ("Process") and drags the items by their handles to reorder
  them; the steps of the
  integrative decision-making process guide, but don't enforce anything.
- **Proposal**: only the secretary captures it from a tension and changes
  it, in the meeting: while it runs, the proposal's page points there,
  and the agenda leads to the proposals' pages only for the others.
  "Discard" withdraws it (e.g. it isn't valid, or its proposer is
  gone); its proposer may "Draft again" on the proposal's page. "Done"
  without adopting or discarding keeps it waiting for the next meeting
  (three months, then it's its proposer's draft again).
- **Objections** aren't recorded: the facilitator asks for them in the
  objection round and tests them, the secretary integrates the valid
  ones by amending the proposal; the notes keep what matters. "Adopt"
  (at the bottom, like "Discard") applies the change right away, the
  mail comes at the end of the meeting.
- **The same role several times**: if a proposal adopted earlier changed
  a role or policy the current one changes too, the meeting shows the
  conflict before adopting. The secretary opens the role in the proposal
  (the pencil next to its name) and merges both versions: text fields contain both between
  markers (`<<<<<<<`, `=======`, `>>>>>>>`), which have to go before
  saving; below the name and the title is the current value, a checkbox
  confirms the merge. If the proposal changes an accountability or domain
  removed in the meantime, its text is among the new ones between
  markers: keep it or delete it. The proposal then starts from the
  current state.
- **Elections**: the facilitator runs the election, the secretary's
  "Record the election" saves the person and the end of the term (Facilitator,
  Secretary, Circle Rep and roles elected by policy). The Circle Rep may
  stay unfilled: "Nobody (Circle Rep only)" ends the Rep's assignment
  without electing anybody. Recording the same
  role again in the meeting replaces the earlier result, as if it had
  never been recorded. Those elected and those replaced hear about it
  when the meeting ends. Due elections are
  marked "election due" in the circle's roles, facilitators get a reminder on Mondays.
  Starting a governance meeting puts them on its agenda ("Election:
  Secretary"), owned by the facilitator; record the election with
  "Record the election", then close the item with "Done".

## Tactical meeting

- **Regular**: "Start a tactical meeting" on the circle's page, every
  member may do that; all of the circle's roles are invited.
- **Ad hoc**: "Call a tactical meeting" in the menu of your name (top
  right): first the roles, also from several circles (the field filters
  them by role, circle, person or focus), then "Next: who is invited"
  lists their Role Leads with their focus (for roles without anybody assigned
  the Circle Leads, marked "As Circle Lead"), all checked; to
  invite only some of them (constitution 3.1), uncheck the others. You
  hold one of the roles at least (or fill one without anybody assigned
  as Circle Lead) and attend. "Start the meeting" starts it. "All meetings" in the menu "More
  actions" (…) of a role's page lists its circle's meetings and those the
  role was invited to.

Lists of meetings show what they were about: the adopted proposals,
else the agenda items.

Steps: check-in, checklists, metrics, updates, agenda, closing. The
updates show the circle's links (e.g. to the task management tool; an
ad hoc meeting's those of the invited roles' circles) and the outcomes of the last three tactical
meetings, to check on them ("Show earlier meetings" for more). For each
agenda item the secretary may record an "Outcome" and a "Link", which
save themselves while typing (the others see them), "Save and done" or
just "Done". The results of the completed items stack up on the right
(scroll up for earlier ones), the agenda strikes them through; clicking
one there shows its result, "Reopen" goes back to it to correct it:

- **Nothing** is fine: many items end with information or a short
  discussion.
- **Next actions and projects** stay in the task management tool, the
  tool doesn't track them. Several of them, e.g. one for everybody
  holding a role, are one link to the to-do list containing them. The
  outcome names the role taking it on with `@` (see
  [finding your way](orientation.md)), e.g. "@Sales sends the offer", and
  the person too when several fill the role and only one of them takes
  it on ("@Sales (Ada) sends the offer"); without, it's all of them.
- **A governance tension** goes into a governance meeting: its owner
  writes it down in a tension ("New proposal" on the circle's page, see [proposals](proposals.md))
  or brings it to the next governance meeting's agenda. The tool can't
  add tensions for somebody else; a to-do reminds them if needed.

The outputs mail at the end lists the items with an outcome or a link.
The Claude Code skill `governance-tactical` (with `gov`, see
[finding your way](orientation.md)) proposes the missing tasks in the task
management tool from the outcomes without a link, with the people holding
the mentioned roles, checks the linked ones, and creates or changes
nothing before you confirmed it.
Tactical meetings imported from GlassFrog have their agenda items only,
GlassFrog's actions and projects aren't imported.
