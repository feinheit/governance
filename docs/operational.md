# Operational work

Some things change directly in the tool, without a proposal:

| What | Who |
| --- | --- |
| Assignments and focus ("Role Leads" on the role's page) | Circle Leads |
| The circle's strategy | Circle Leads |
| Metrics | Circle Leads |
| Checklist items, links and notes | everybody |
| Policies of a domain | whoever holds the role with the domain |
| Settings (deadline for async proposals, roles elected by policy) | Secretary |

- Notes keep what helps doing a role's work, e.g. a checklist or how
  something is ordered, in Markdown; "Links and notes" on the role's
  page, in any order, a click unfolds a note. Like links, they're in the history on request,
  nobody gets a mail about them.
- A circle may have several Circle Leads, each of them may do all of
  this. The Circle Leads of a sub-circle are assigned by the Circle Leads
  of the circle above; while nobody leads a sub-circle, the Circle Leads
  of the circle above lead it (constitution 1.4.2). Elected roles are
  elected, not assigned.
- A Circle Lead can't be the circle's Circle Rep (constitution 5.1.1).
  When an assignment or its removal would make a Circle Rep lead their
  circle, also from the circle above, the tool warns and then ends the
  Circle Rep's assignment; the circle elects a new one. A proposal
  moving a circle shows it before it's adopted, deactivating somebody
  ends it without asking.
- Whoever wants to give up a role turns to the Circle Leads, who remove
  them, also from elected roles ("Remove" under "Role Leads" on the role's
  page).
- The secretary may do everything in the tool the Circle Leads may, so
  that there's a stand-in.
- Admins may do all of this everywhere, also run any circle's meetings,
  e.g. in the top circle, which has no Circle Leads above it. Buttons
  for what the constitution leaves to others are amber for admins and
  for the Secretary standing in ("As admin" when hovering them), and
  their forms say so: only use them when you know it's needed. Buttons
  deleting or removing something are red and ask first.
- When a role becomes a circle, its policies become the circle's, which
  change through its governance. When it turns back into a role, the
  policies for its domains stay and its Role Leads change them again, the
  others are archived.
- The settings implement what the circle's policies define. Sub-circles
  without a deadline for async proposals of their own use the one of the
  circle above, so the policy setting it should say that it applies to
  all sub-circles (constitution 5.2.1). Without any, async proposals are
  only adopted once everybody consented. The field shows what applies
  while it's empty.
- The tool records all these changes.

The admins add new people and deactivate people who leave: their roles
end, their proposals stay.
